# Channel research — where 3–6 picture-book buyers actually are

**Status: working hypothesis, not verified.** Desk research from platform mechanics and niche
patterns. Not measured live. Verify before it goes into a published calendar.

## The core correction
Organic social is a weak *direct* sales channel for picture books. Parents buy from Amazon search,
cover thumbnails, reviews and recommendation. Social's job at $0 is:
1. **Pinterest** — compounding search traffic (the only channel where an hour keeps paying).
2. **The first 20–50 honest reviews** on the Amazon listing (real readers only; incentivised or
   fabricated reviews are a rules violation and an account risk).
3. **An email list** you own, so book two doesn't start from zero.

"Post everywhere daily" burns the hours budget for almost nothing. Pinterst-first, others satellites.

## Platform calls
- **Pinterest — priority, ~50–60% of effort.** Vertical 2:3 (1000×1500) pins with the search query
  *on the image*. Keywords in pin title, description, board name and alt text — that is what gets
  indexed. Hashtags: skip. 3–5 fresh pins/week, scheduled 2–4 weeks ahead with Pinterst's free
  native scheduler. Boards themed on parent *moments* ("Bedtime read-alouds", "Rainbow & weather
  crafts"), not on "my book". Best demographic fit of the four.
- **Facebook — the grandparent channel, via Groups not your Page.** A new Page reaches almost
  nobody. Join 5–10 local parent / grandparent / kids-book-recommendation groups, participate
  genuinely for weeks, only self-promote where rules allow, never copy-paste across groups.
  Photo + short personal story beats link posts. Hashtags: dead here.
- **Instagram — credibility base, not a discovery engine.** Reels are the only real non-follower
  reach. Useful carousels ("5 things to say instead of 'hurry up', plus the book we read after").
  3–5 specific hashtags max; write captions with searchable phrases instead. 2–3 Reels + 1 carousel
  a week. Free multiplier: **Collab posts** with other small kidlit/mom accounts.
- **TikTik — highest reach, worst fit. Cap it.** 8-week experiment, 3–5 posts/week, fixed hour
  budget, then kill it if it isn't sending site clicks. Uncapped it quietly eats the whole project.

## Search phrases with real buyer intent (use verbatim)
"bedtime books for 3 year olds" · "books for 4 year old boys" · "books about big feelings for
toddlers" · "preschool books about weather" · "rainbow book for kids" · "toddler coloring book" ·
"jumbo coloring book for kids" · "coloring book with thick lines" · "gifts for a 4 year old" ·
"grandparent gift from grandchild".
Seasonal: Nov–Dec gifting peak (start pinning 6–8 weeks before), Aug back-to-school, June summer reading.

**Strongest hypothesis:** activity/roundup content where the book is *one item in a list*
("rainbow crafts for prescholars") beats "buy my book" content. Unmeasured but well grounded.

## Cut outright
30-hashtag sets and hashtag research on any platform · same asset cross-posted everywhere ·
Facebook Page organic posting to a near-zero following · follow-for-follow, engagement pods,
bought followers · follow-to-enter giveaways (against FB/IG rules, attracts prize-hunters) ·
trend-audio jumping · mailmerge collab blasts.

## Weekly rhythm (~4–6 focused hours)
Pinterest 3–5 pins (scheduled ahead) · IG 2–3 Reels + 1 carousel · FB 2–3 group posts ·
TikTik capped experiment. Canva free tier for design, CapCut free tier for edits. No paid tool needed.

## Open dependancies
Real book blurb, cover art, confirmed age range, and the Amazon ASINs — none are in the shared
folder yet. Pin and caption copy cannot be finalised without them.
